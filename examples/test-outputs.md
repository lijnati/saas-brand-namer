# Test 1
Prompt: "I'm building a waitlist + referral leaderboard micro-SaaS for indie hackers. Want a name that feels like Linear or Raycast."

Naming for: waitlist pages with a referral leaderboard for indie launches | Feel: fast, minimal, a little hype

## Top picks

**1. Stanchion** (STAN-shun) · unexpected concrete noun
Why it fits: the brass post that holds the velvet rope outside a club. It is the object that turns a queue into an event, which is the whole product.
Watch out: 9 letters, and some people will misspell it on first hearing.
Domains to try: stanchion.dev, getstanchion.com, stanchion.so

**2. Barnum** · proper noun
Why it fits: P.T. Barnum basically invented manufactured hype. A referral leaderboard is that idea, automated.
Watch out: "there's a sucker born every minute" baggage for some audiences.
Domains to try: barnum.dev, usebarnum.com

**3. Matinee** · repurposed real word
Why it fits: the early showing before the main release, which is exactly what a waitlist is.
Watch out: common name for cafes and cinemas; spelling with or without the accent.
Domains to try: matinee.so, getmatinee.com

## More options
- **Marquee**: the lit sign announcing what is coming
- **Prelude**: the part before the main thing, soft and premium
- **Stub**: the ticket stub; short, punchy, very ownable as a verb
- **Wick**: the slow burn before launch; four letters
- **Baton**: passed from person to person, like a referral
- **Foyer**: where people wait before the doors open
- **Usher**: shows people to their seat (flag: the singer)
- **Overture**: the opening that builds anticipation

Skipped on purpose: Waitly, LaunchPad, HypeHub, Queueio

# Test 2
Prompt: "name ideas for a B2B tool that helps African SMEs reconcile mobile money payments (M-Pesa, Telebirr) with their books"

Naming for: mobile money reconciliation for East African SMEs | Feel: trustworthy, simple, local but global-ready

## Top picks

**1. Sawa** (SAH-wah) · foreign word (Swahili)
Why it fits: means "equal", "same", "okay". Two columns that are sawa are reconciled. Instantly familiar across East Africa and two clean syllables anywhere else.
Watch out: widely used by local businesses, so expect to hunt for a domain variant.
Domains to try: sawa.app, getsawa.com, sawa.so

**2. Mizani** (mee-ZAH-nee) · foreign word (Swahili, from Arabic)
Why it fits: the scale or balance. Amharic speakers know the same root (mizan) as balance, so it lands in both Kenya and Ethiopia.
Watch out: three syllables; check the spelling people expect in each market.
Domains to try: mizani.app, usemizani.com

**3. Pacioli** (pah-CHO-lee) · proper noun
Why it fits: Luca Pacioli published the first description of double-entry bookkeeping in 1494. Accountants recognize it; everyone else hears a warm Italian name.
Watch out: hard to spell for people outside finance.
Domains to try: pacioli.dev, getpacioli.com

## More options
- **Plumb**: a plumb line shows what is true and level
- **Notch**: the cut on a tally stick, the oldest ledger
- **Hesabu**: Swahili for accounts or arithmetic
- **Seam**: where two sides meet cleanly
- **Bead**: the abacus bead, small and tactile
- **Even**: what the books are when you're done (flag: several fintechs)
- **Keel**: keeps the business steady
- **Settle**: the action itself, plain and confident

Dropped for collisions: Square (Block), Stitch (South African payments API), Tally (form builder)
Skipped on purpose: LedgerLink, PayBridge, Ledgerly, Balancio

# Test 3
Prompt: "We're calling our AI API cost monitoring tool TokenGuard. Is that good? Give me alternatives."

Verdict on TokenGuard: not recommended. It is the exact pattern this skill exists to avoid: two words glued in CamelCase, purely descriptive, and "Guard" appears in hundreds of security and cost tools. The .com and a clean trademark will both be hard.

Naming for: spend caps and alerts for AI API usage | Feel: calm, in control, engineering-grade

## Top picks

**1. Weir** (rhymes with "ear") · repurposed real word
Why it fits: a low dam that lets water through at a controlled rate instead of blocking it. That is exactly what a token budget does to API calls.
Watch out: fails the radio test for some people ("wier", "we're"); fine in writing.
Domains to try: weir.dev, getweir.com

**2. Governor** · repurposed real word
Why it fits: an engine governor caps speed automatically. This caps spend automatically. Engineers get it immediately.
Watch out: political connotation; 8 letters.
Domains to try: governor.dev, usegovernor.com

**3. Ballast** · repurposed real word
Why it fits: the weight that keeps a ship from tipping, the way cost controls keep an AI feature from sinking the budget.
Watch out: slightly heavy, slow feel.
Domains to try: ballast.dev, getballast.com

## More options
- **Sluice**: a gate that controls flow
- **Damper**: reduces oscillation; calms spiky usage
- **Spigot**: you decide how far it opens
- **Ohm**: the unit of resistance; three letters
- **Keel**: stability under load
- **Rheostat**: a dial for current (long, but very engineer)
- **Toll**: every request pays its way
- **Throttle**: precise but crowded as a name

Skipped on purpose: CostPilot, Spendwise, Budgetly, Sentinel
